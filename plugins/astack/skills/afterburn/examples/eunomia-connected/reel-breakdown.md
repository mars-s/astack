# Reference reel: keyframe breakdown

Source: instagram.com/reel/Dcn6EINyzaa ("A personal motion project for OpenAI", launchdsigns). 31.0 s, 1280x720, 30 fps, music only.

Read at 10 fps for the whole film, 30 fps across every transition, and full size at eight key frames.

## The numbers that matter

- About 26 distinct beats in 31 s. A new idea lands every 1.2 s on average.
- Almost no plain fades. Nearly every transition turns one thing into the next thing (text becomes a line, dots become a ring, a button sprays out cards).
- The camera never sits still. Every shot has a push, a pan, a tilt or a rack focus.
- Fast moves carry motion blur. Small things glow. Depth is shown with blur (things far away or very close are soft).
- Three different worlds: dark tech (0 to 7.6 s), bright airy UI (7.6 to 21 s), warm natural (21 to 31 s). The colour story goes dark, then light, then warm.

## Why my first Eunomia cut looked cheap

1. **Fades instead of transformations.** Mine crossfades between scenes. The reel turns each thing into the next.
2. **A frozen camera.** Mine had a couple of slow zooms. The reel moves in every shot.
3. **No optics.** No motion blur, no depth of field, almost no bloom, no grain. These are what make it feel filmed.
4. **Stock-looking stills where the reel has real material.** The reel's meadow and forest move. Its cards are real poster art. Its tree is a painted illustration with blossoms. Its rings are 3D with glossy light.
5. **Text with no life.** The reel types letters in, flashes a bloom when a word lands, glitches for 1 or 2 frames, tints new letters, tracks letters apart on exit.
6. **Half the beats.** Mine had about 10 beats in 39 s. The reel has about 26 in 31 s.
